# Speaker scripts, one card per person

Print this and hand each speaker their own card. Six cards, six speakers, roughly 90
seconds of speech each.

The read passage is **identical for everyone**, because it is the only cross-speaker
control in the set. The other three prompts are different per speaker so the
spontaneous content varies.

Session setup, room choice and the rules for the person running the session are in
[`RECORDING_SESSIONS.md`](RECORDING_SESSIONS.md). File format and labelling are in
[`BENCHMARK_FORMAT.md`](BENCHMARK_FORMAT.md).

---

## For the speaker, read this first

- **Speak normally.** Not a newsreader voice, not a performance, not a phone-call
  voice. Your ordinary speaking voice is the entire point.
- **Do not put on an accent**, and do not try to sound "neutral". Say names and words
  the way you normally say them.
- **Pauses are fine.** Two or three seconds of thinking is realistic and useful. Do
  not fill them with "umm" on purpose, and do not edit them out afterwards either.
- **If you stumble, keep going.** Do not restart unless the take is unusable. Real
  calls contain stumbles.
- **It is one continuous recording.** Do not stop between takes. Someone will say the
  take number, then you wait about **3 seconds in silence** before starting, and stay
  silent for about **3 seconds** after finishing. A gap goes around **every** take,
  not just the first and last. Those gaps are how the recording gets split up
  afterwards, and they are also the only measurement of the room's background noise,
  so do not skip them and do not talk through them.
- **The first 5 seconds are silence.** Nobody speaks at all. That is deliberate.
- **Nothing personal.** No real phone numbers, OTPs, account numbers, addresses or
  anyone's full name, in any section, including the unscripted ones.

Your voice may be used to build a synthetic copy for testing. That is a separate
permission and you will be asked for it explicitly. Recording does not imply it.

## Card 1

**Speaker ID:** `spk_01`

### Take 1, read aloud (target 25 s)

> Good morning. My name is Ravi Sharma and I am calling from the district office
> near the railway station. The train number is one two four seven and it reaches
> the city at half past six. Last month the total came to two lakh forty thousand
> rupees. Could you confirm whether the meeting is on Thursday or Friday? Thank you
> very much.

### Take 2, speak freely (target 25 s)

> Describe how you travel to college, step by step, from leaving home to arriving.

### Take 3, speak with urgency (target 20 s)

> You are twenty minutes late to meet a friend who is waiting. Explain why, as if you
> are hurrying there right now.

### Take 4, mix your languages (target 20 s)

> Explain what the passage in Take 1 was about to a family member, the way you would
> actually say it at home. Do not keep it in one language.

## Extra take: clone source

**Only for the one or two speakers whose voice the demo clones, and only with their
separate, explicit consent.**

Roughly 60 seconds, recorded as its own file, and **not** part of the 90 seconds
above. It must be different audio from the four takes, because building a clone from
a recording and then testing the detector against that same recording measures
nothing.

Content can be anything from the cards above, or simply:

> Talk for a minute about anything you like: your day, your commute, a place you have
> been. Ordinary speaking voice, no performance.

---

## Why these four and not a scam script

Nobody reads a scam script. The transcript channel is a separate concern and is Round
2, and it has nothing to do with whether a voice is synthetic.

More importantly, **these voices get cloned.** A synthetic copy of your teammate
saying "transfer the money now, do not hang up" is not a test asset, it is a working
attack tool that outlives the hackathon. Take 3 is built to produce urgent
*prosody*, which is what might trip a false warning, without producing urgent
*instructions*, which is what a fraudster would want.
